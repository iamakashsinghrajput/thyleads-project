# Communication Style

## Internal (working with Claude)
- Tone: casual and direct
- Format: short paragraphs and bullet points

## LinkedIn Posts
- Tone: thought-leader style, backed by data or specific insight
- No emojis
- No em dashes
- No jargon or buzzwords
- Do not open with AI-like frames ("In today's fast-paced world...", "As we navigate...", "It's no secret that...", "Let's be honest...", "Here's the thing...")
- Write like a sharp operator sharing a real observation, not a content marketer performing expertise

## Blog Posts
- Tone: conversational and data-driven
- No emojis
- No em dashes
- No jargon
- Back every claim with data, a specific example, or clear reasoning
- Write like you're explaining something useful to a smart peer

## LinkedIn Comment Drafts

1. Remove every "importance" sentence. If it talks about impact, legacy, significance, or broader trends, delete it.
2. Kill AI vocabulary. Banned words: pivotal, crucial, enhance, underscore, landscape, vibrant, testament, foster, delve, showcase, intricate, evolving.
3. Delete all contrast structures. No "It's not X, it's Y." No "Not A. Not B. But C." No "Despite this..."
4. Cut all vague authority. No "experts say," "industry reports," "many believe."
5. Remove summary endings. No "In conclusion," "Overall," or neat wrap-ups.
6. Replace general claims with specifics. If it says "improves results," say how. If there's no proof, cut the line.
7. Shorten sentences over 16 words.
8. If a sentence could apply to 1,000 other topics, delete it.
9. No motivational tone. No TED Talk energy. No preaching.
10. Sound like a message sent to one person, not a stage speech.
11. For the "disagree intelligently" pillar: always soften the tone. Never challenge the author directly. Frame as a suggestion, a personal experience, or a constructive curious question.

## Always
- Never use emojis in professional content
- Avoid jargon and buzzwords
- No em dashes -- use a comma or restructure the sentence
- Do not start with AI-sounding openers
